Samsung did something unusual with its 2026 watches. The Galaxy Watch Ultra 2 and the Galaxy Watch 9, both launched on July 22 at Unpacked in London, run the exact same chip. Same Snapdragon Wear Elite, same 2GB of RAM, same Wear OS 7. Open an app on either one and you will wait the same amount of time.

That makes this comparison simpler than most, and harder for the expensive watch. The Ultra 2 costs $699.99. The Watch 9 starts at $379.99. The $320 gap buys you battery, brightness, toughness, and dive tracking. It does not buy you speed. If you were hoping the pricier watch would feel faster, it will not, and that fact decides this comparison for most people.

## The same chip changes the question

Samsung spent years running its own Exynos chips in Galaxy Watches. Both 2026 models switch to Qualcomm's Snapdragon Wear Elite, a 3nm five-core chip with a dedicated Hexagon NPU for on-device AI. It is the first time Samsung has put the same silicon in both tiers, and it flattens the usual flagship argument. Raise-to-talk Gemini, smooth scrolling, and app launches are identical on both watches. The only performance difference on paper is storage: 64GB on the Ultra 2 against 32GB on the Watch 9, which matters only if you download a lot of music or maps for offline use.

So the real question is not which watch is faster. It is whether you will use the hardware the Ultra 2 wraps around that chip.

## Battery is the Ultra 2's best argument

This is where the money shows up. The Ultra 2 packs an 800mAh battery, 35 percent larger than the original Ultra's, and Samsung rates it at up to 60 hours with the always-on display on and 80 hours with it off. The Watch 9 manages up to 30 hours with always-on, from a 390mAh cell in the 40mm model and 445mAh in the 44mm. In practice that is the difference between charging every night and charging twice a week, and between tracking a full weekend of hiking without a charger and packing one.

The Ultra 2 also charges faster. Samsung added high-frequency fast wireless charging that it says is exclusive to the Ultra 2 among its watches. It is a small thing until you are topping up before a run and the Watch 9 is still crawling toward full.

One honest caveat: these are Samsung's numbers, not independent tests. Battery life on any watch depends on how many workouts you track, whether LTE is on, and how often the screen wakes. But the gap between 800mAh and 390mAh is too large for real-world use to erase.

## Brightness, size, and the wrist test

The Ultra 2's display peaks at 5,000 nits, the brightest panel on any smartwatch right now, against 3,000 nits on the Watch 9. Both are readable in direct sunlight, so the extra 2,000 nits matters most when you are mid-workout, sweating, and glancing at the screen at an angle. It is a real advantage, just a narrow one.

Size is the bigger everyday difference, and it cuts both ways. The Ultra 2 comes in one size, 47mm, and weighs 61.5g. It has presence on the wrist, which is a polite way of saying it is a big watch. The Watch 9 comes in 40mm and 44mm, weighs 31.5g or 34g, and at 8.6mm thick it slips under a shirt cuff without a fight. If you have slim wrists or you sleep in your watch, try the Ultra 2 on before you buy it. A watch you take off at night is a watch that stops tracking your sleep.

## Durability and the outdoors

The Ultra 2 is the only one of the pair built for genuinely rough use. Its titanium case carries an IP69K rating for dust and high-pressure water jets, 10 ATM water resistance, MIL-STD-810H testing, and EN13319 certification as a dive instrument, rated to 40 metres. Samsung worked with Mares on dive tracking that logs depth, water temperature, and dive time in real time. Trail runners get route tracking with turn-by-turn cues, elevation data, and a hydration alert, plus direct pairing with power meters and speed sensors, with FTP calculated on the wrist.

The Watch 9 is no slouch: IP68, 5 ATM, and MIL-STD-810H, which covers rain, sweat, and the occasional swim. But it is not a dive computer and it is not built to be knocked against rock. If your weekends involve a wetsuit or a trailhead, the Ultra 2 earns its keep. If they involve a gym and a commute, the Watch 9's durability is already more than enough.

## Health tracking is nearly identical

Both watches share Samsung's BioActive sensor and the same health suite: sleep tracking with a Sleep Apnea feature, Vitals, Heart Health Score, Daily Cardio Load, and a Fitness Index. Nothing in the health department separates them.

There is one catch worth knowing before you buy either one, and it has nothing to do with which model you pick. ECG and blood pressure still only work when the watch is paired with a Samsung phone. Pair either watch with a Pixel or another Android phone and those two features stay locked. Samsung has kept this restriction for years, and it is the single most annoying thing about an otherwise open Wear OS watch.

## Black Friday prices, checked November 24

Black Friday is November 27, and the two watches are having very different sales. Samsung's own site has the Watch 9 at $269, $110 off its $379.99 list price, plus a $50 instant credit for accessories, according to [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/17/galaxy-watch-9-deal-269-dollars/?extended-comments=1). Amazon has it at $373.99, or $379 with a $50 gift card thrown in.

The Ultra 2, meanwhile, is still sitting at its $699.99 list price at Amazon and Best Buy as of November 19, with no discount in sight in the US. In the UK, Samsung offers £100 off the Ultra 2 with code BFW until December 1. Trade-ins can cut the Ultra 2 deeply if you have an original Ultra to hand over, with some buyers reporting as little as $199 after trade-in on Samsung's app, but those deals look targeted and inconsistent.

Do the math at street prices and the gap is not $320. It is $430: $269 for the Watch 9 against $699.99 for the Ultra 2. That is a lot of money for battery and brightness you might never miss.

## Who should buy which

Buy the Watch 9 if you want a smartwatch for daily life: notifications, sleep, workouts, and a screen you can read outside. At $269 this Black Friday it is the best value in Samsung's lineup, and the same chip means you give up nothing in everyday speed.

Buy the Ultra 2 if you can name the feature that justifies it before you check out. Multi-day battery on back-to-back trail days, a dive computer on your wrist, a screen you can read at noon on a mountain. If you cannot name it, you are paying $430 extra for a tougher case and a longer charge interval, and you will notice the weight on your wrist far more often than you notice either.

Same brain, different body. Most wrists want the cheaper one.
