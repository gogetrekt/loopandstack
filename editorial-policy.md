This page explains how Loop and Stack content is researched, written, checked, and corrected. It applies to every article on the site.

## Sourcing standards

Every article starts with primary sources: official documentation, changelogs, GitHub releases, and vendor pricing pages. Secondary reporting can point us to a fact, but a claim only runs when we can trace it to a primary source or test it ourselves. Sources are linked at the end of each article.

Prices, plan limits, and version numbers are checked on the day an article is published. When an article is updated, those details are checked again and the article's updated date changes.

We synthesize several sources and add our own structure, testing notes, and judgment. We do not copy or paraphrase other publications' articles, and we do not present another writer's testing as our own.

## How reviews and comparisons work

Reviews and comparisons follow a fixed format: a quick verdict, pros and cons, the specifics that matter in daily use, current pricing, and a clear statement of who a tool is for and who should skip it.

Rankings are decided on features, pricing, reliability, and fit for the reader. Affiliate payouts, advertising relationships, and vendor requests never influence a ranking or a verdict. Vendors cannot pay for coverage, and we do not accept sponsored reviews, paid placements, or review units with conditions attached. See our [Affiliate Disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/).

Where we have not been able to test a tool hands-on, the article says so and sticks to what the documentation and verifiable facts support.

## AI assistance

Our writers use AI tools for research assistance and drafting, as most modern newsrooms use search engines and spellcheckers. Every article is edited, fact-checked against its sources, and approved by the writer named on it before publication. Any claim we cannot verify does not run, no matter how it was drafted.

## Authorship and accountability

Articles are published under editorial pen names, listed with their beats on the [About](/about/) page. The writer named on an article is accountable for its accuracy, and the byline stays on the article when it is updated.

## Corrections and updates

If we get something wrong, we fix the article and, for anything material, note the correction in the article itself. Readers can flag errors by email at nethroes@proton.me or by opening an issue on our GitHub repository at github.com/gogetrekt/loopandstack.

When a tool changes its pricing or features, we update the existing article and its date rather than publishing a duplicate, so links readers have saved keep pointing at current information.

## Conflicts of interest

Writers do not cover tools they have a financial interest in beyond the site's disclosed affiliate relationships. If a conflict ever becomes unavoidable, it will be disclosed in the article.
