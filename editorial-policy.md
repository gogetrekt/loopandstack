How Loop and Stack content is made, checked, and corrected.

## How we research

Every article starts with primary sources: official documentation, changelogs, GitHub releases, and vendor pricing pages. Prices, plan limits, and version numbers are checked on the day an article is published or updated, and the sources are linked at the end of the article.

We synthesize several sources and add our own structure, testing notes, and judgment. We do not copy or paraphrase other publications' articles.

## How we review

Reviews and comparisons follow a fixed format: a quick verdict, pros and cons, the specifics that matter in daily use, current pricing, and a clear statement of who a tool is for and who should skip it. Rankings are decided on features, pricing, and fit for the reader, never on affiliate payouts. See our [Affiliate Disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/).

## AI assistance

Our writers use AI tools for research assistance and drafting, the way most modern newsrooms use spellcheckers and search. Every article is edited, fact-checked against its sources, and approved by the writer named on it before publication. Claims we cannot verify do not run.

## Authorship

Articles are published under editorial pen names. Each writer covers a fixed beat, listed on our [About](/about/) page, and is responsible for the accuracy of what runs under their name.

## Corrections and updates

If we get something wrong, we fix the article and note the change. Readers can flag errors by opening an issue on our GitHub repository at github.com/gogetrekt/loopandstack. When a tool changes its pricing or features, we update the article and its date rather than publishing a duplicate.
